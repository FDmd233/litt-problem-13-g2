\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:orthogonal} and \eqref{eq:connected-irreducible}, the connected
centre of $G^0$ acts by orthogonal scalars and is therefore trivial.  Thus
$G^0$ is semisimple.  An irreducible representation of a product of
semisimple groups is an external tensor product.  Since $7$ is prime and the
action is faithful, $G^0$ has one simple factor.

For a dominant weight $\lambda$, the Weyl dimension formula is
\[
 \dim V_\lambda=
 \prod_{\alpha>0}
 \frac{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle}
      {\langle\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle}
\]
(compare \cite[Lecture~24]{FultonHarris1991}).  It is strictly increasing when a nonzero
dominant weight is added.  Applying it to the fundamental weights of each
simple type gives the following complete seven-dimensional list:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{type}&\text{highest weight}&\text{dimension}\\ \hline
A_1&6\omega_1&7\\
A_6&\omega_1,\ \omega_6&7\\
B_3&\omega_1&7\\
G_2&\omega_1&7.
\end{array}
\]
The remaining types are excluded as follows.  For $A_r$ the
fundamental dimensions are $\binom{r+1}{i}$.  The only ranks below $A_6$
that could conceivably contribute dimension seven are $A_2,A_3,A_4,A_5$.
The Weyl formula gives, respectively, the dimensions below the next jump as
$3,3,6,6$ (then $8$), $4,4,6$ (then $10$), $5,5$ (then $10$), and $6,6$
(then $15$).  Thus none has a seven-dimensional simple module.  For
$r\ge7$ even the standard $A_r$-module has dimension at least $8$.
For $C_2$ the fundamental dimensions are $4,5$ and the next irreducible
dimension is $10$; for $C_3$ they are $6,14,14$ and
$\dim V_{2\omega_1}=21$; for $C_r$ with $r\ge4$ the standard module
already has dimension at least $8$.  In type $B_r$, $r\ge4$, the vector
module has dimension at least $9$, and in type $D_r$, $r\ge4$, it has
dimension at least $8$; the spin modules do not produce dimension $7$.
Finally, the smallest nontrivial irreducible dimensions of
$F_4,E_6,E_7,E_8$ are $26,27,56,248$, whereas $G_2$ has its
seven-dimensional fundamental module.  This leaves exactly the four cases
in the table.

The orthogonal self-duality of $V$ excludes type $A_6$.  In type $A_1$, the
central element $-I\in\mathrm{SL}_2$ acts trivially on $\Sym^6(\CC^2)$,
so the faithful image is $\mathrm{PGL}_2$; in type $B_3$, the
seven-dimensional vector representation has faithful image $\mathrm{SO}_7$.
The remaining possibilities are therefore $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ on
$\Sym^6(\CC^2)$, $\mathrm{SO}_7$ on its standard module, and $G_2$ on
$V_7$.  For $\mathrm{SO}_7$, the exterior
square of the standard module is the irreducible adjoint module, so
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{SO}_7}(V,\Lambda^2V)=0$, contrary to
\eqref{eq:hom-exterior}.  For a nontrivial cocharacter of $\mathrm{PGL}_2$, the weights on
$\Sym^6(\CC^2)$ are seven distinct numbers.  With the primitive
cocharacter of the adjoint torus (so that the usual $\mathrm{SL}_2$ weights
are divided by two), they are
\[
 -3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,
\]
and every other nonzero cocharacter multiplies them by a nonzero integer.
The trivial cocharacter has one weight of multiplicity seven.  Neither
possibility agrees with \eqref{eq:hodge-weights}.  Hence $G^0=G_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The full Tannaka group]\label[corollary]{cor:full-g2}
There is an isomorphism of pairs
\[
 (G,V)\simeq(G_2,V_7).
\]
In particular, $G$ is connected; this identifies the full Tannaka group, not
only its identity component.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:connected-g2}, $G^0\simeq G_2$ on $V_7$.  Since
$G^0\mathrel{\triangleleft}G\subset \mathrm{O}(V)$,
\[
 G\subset N_{\mathrm{O}(V)}(G_2)=G_2\times\{\pm I\}.
\]
Indeed, conjugation gives a homomorphism from the normalizer to
$\operatorname{Aut}(G_2)$.  Since $\operatorname{Out}(G_2)=1$, multiplication
by a suitable element of $G_2$ puts every normalizing element in the
centralizer.  Schur's lemma makes that centralizer scalar, and its intersection
with $\mathrm{O}(V)$ is $\{\pm I\}$.  If $G\ne G^0$, choose
$g\in G\cap(-G_2)$ and write $g=(-I)h$ with $h\in G_2=G^0$.  Then
$-I=gh^{-1}\in G$.  But $-I$ acts by $-1$ on $V$ and by $+1$ on
$\Lambda^2V$, contradicting \eqref{eq:hom-exterior}.  Hence $G=G^0=G_2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Specialization and an explicit example}

\begin{proposition}[Specialization of the intersection complex]
\label{prop:specialization}
For every $b\in\cB(\CC)$ there is a smooth pointed curve through $b$,
with generic point in $\cB^\circ$, along which
\[
 \psi(\IC_{X_{\bar\eta}})\simeq\IC_{X_b}.
\]
Consequently the specialization Tannaka group embeds as a closed subgroup
of $G_2$, compatibly with the seven-dimensional tautological modules.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a smooth curve $T\subset\cB$ through $b$ whose generic point lies
in $\cB^\circ$, and pass to the strict henselization at $b$.
Let $\cN_T$ be the relative norm fibre in $\cC_T^{(3)}$, and let
$D_0$ be the section consisting of the three ramification points.
Set $\wh{\cN}_T=\operatorname{Bl}_{D_0}\cN_T$.

This is a smooth proper family of surfaces.  To check the assertion at
the centre, label the branch points after an \'etale base change.
An additive local coordinate on the elliptic curve and cube-root
coordinates at the ramification points put the relative norm equation
in the form
\[
 z_1^3+z_2^3+z_3^3=0
\]
over the base.  Indeed the three elliptic coordinates sum to zero at
$D_0$, and the local ramification index is three at each point.
The relative germ is therefore the product of the base with the cone
over the Fermat plane cubic.  Blowing up its vertex gives a smooth
family and commutes with passage to fibres.  Away from $D_0$ the norm
fibres are smooth by \cref{prop:theta-resolution}; the relative norm
fibre is flat, being an effective Cartier divisor in the smooth
relative symmetric cube.  Thus it is smooth there as well.

The Abel map gives a projective morphism
$\wh j:\wh{\cN}_T\to\cP_T$.  On each fibre it is birational onto $X_t$
and an isomorphism away from zero.  Its exceptional curves are the
elliptic curve $E_0$ and the strict transform $\wt\Gamma$ of $|\kappa|$.
As in \cref{prop:theta-resolution}, their intersection matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-3&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det=2.
\]
The semismall decomposition consequently gives, on both the geometric
generic and closed fibres,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-two-skyscrapers}
 R\wh j_{t,*}\QQ_{\wh N_t}[2]
       \simeq\IC_{X_t}\oplus\delta_0^{\oplus2}.
\end{equation}
The classes of the two relative exceptional curves supply constant
skyscraper summands.  Smooth base change for $\wh{\cN}_T\to T$ and
proper base change for $\wh j$ identify the nearby cycles of the left
side with the corresponding closed-fibre direct image.
Nearby cycles are perverse exact and send $\delta_0$ to $\delta_0$.
Applying them to \eqref{eq:relative-two-skyscrapers} and cancelling the
two skyscraper summands proves the asserted isomorphism.  Cancellation
is valid because the closed-fibre direct image is semisimple, so the
nearby intersection complex is a direct summand of a semisimple object.

For an abelian scheme in characteristic zero, nearby cycles preserve
convolution, hypercohomology and character twists, and the specialization
map on fundamental groups is an isomorphism.
\cite[Lemma~14.1 and Section~14]{KraemerWeissauer2015} therefore gives
\[
 G(\IC_{X_b})\hookrightarrow G(\IC_{X_{\bar\eta}}).
\]
Here one may spread the curve and construction over a finitely generated
subfield of $\CC$, use characteristic-zero \'etale nearby cycles, and
then use comparison with complex sheaves.  Invariance under extension of
algebraically closed fields
\cite[Corollary~4.4]{JKLM2025} identifies the generic group with the
group computed in \cref{cor:full-g2}.  The fibre functors in specialization
identify their tautological modules.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All allowed parameters]\label{thm:all-parameters}
For every $b\in\cB(\CC)$,
\[
 \bigl(G(\IC_{X_b}),\omega(\IC_{X_b})\bigr)\simeq(G_2,V_7).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $H=G(\IC_{X_b})$.  By \cref{prop:specialization}, $H\subset G_2$
on $V_7$.  The geometry and Hodge calculation of Section~3 apply to
every $b\in\cB$.  In particular, the translation stabilizer is trivial,
$V_7|_{H^0}$ is irreducible, and the antidiagonal Hodge cocharacter has
weights $-2,0,2$ of multiplicities $2,3,2$ in $[H^0,H^0]$.
The determinant argument preceding \cref{prop:connected-g2} uses only
these properties and applies unchanged.

The orthogonal seven-dimensional list in the proof of
\cref{prop:connected-g2} leaves $\mathrm{PGL}_2$, $\mathrm{SO}_7$ and
$G_2$ as possibilities for $H^0$.  The Hodge weights exclude
$\mathrm{PGL}_2$, and $\dim\mathrm{SO}_7=21>14=\dim G_2$ excludes
$\mathrm{SO}_7\subset G_2$.  Hence $H^0=G_2$, and
$G_2=H^0\subset H\subset G_2$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:main}]
The relative construction in \cref{sec:pair-splitting} gives a symmetric
relatively ample effective Cartier divisor, flat with geometrically
integral fibres.  The polarization and resolution are those of
\cref{prop:theta-resolution}; \cref{cor:theta-ic} gives
$\chi(\IC_{X_b})=7$.  The Tannaka pair is determined by
\cref{thm:all-parameters}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A rational example]
The smooth cover
\[
 E:y^2=x^3+2,\qquad C:w^3=y-1
\]
and its centred Prym theta surface, defined over $\QQ$, realize
$(G_2,V_7)$ after base change to $\CC$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Here $\Delta_E=108$, $\Delta_{\mathrm{br}}=27$, and $c=1$.
Apply \cref{thm:all-parameters}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Minimal ambient dimension for $V_7$]
No intersection complex of an integral subvariety in an abelian variety
of dimension at most two has the Tannaka pair $(G_2,V_7)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A point has Euler characteristic one.  For an integral curve with
normalization of genus $g$, its perverse intersection complex has Euler
characteristic $2g-2$, which is even.  A two-dimensional integral
subvariety of an abelian surface is the whole surface, with Euler
characteristic zero.  None gives the required dimension seven.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Normalized conic coefficients}
\label{app:normalized-conic}
On the normalized chart $e_1=v=1$ the shifted curve is
\begin{equation}
 Y^2+2cY
 =\lambda x^3+\bigl(1-\lambda(1-3e_2+3e_3)\bigr)x^2
 +\bigl(2c+\lambda(e_2^3-3e_2e_3+3e_3^2)\bigr)x
 -\lambda e_3^3.
\label{eq:appendix-shifted-curve}
\end{equation}
For the section $s=us_0+s_1+zs_2$ one has
\[
 \alpha=u(x-Y)+(Y-e_3)-zY,\qquad
 \beta=e_2+z(x-e_3),\qquad
 \gamma=-1+e_2z.
\]
Substitute these expressions into
$\operatorname{Nm}(s)=\alpha^3+\beta^3Y+\gamma^3Y^2-3\alpha\beta\gamma Y$,
reduce $Y^2$ using \eqref{eq:appendix-shifted-curve}, and divide by the fixed
line $Y-x$.  The quotient
$Q_s=q_{00}+q_{01}x+q_{02}Y+q_{11}x^2+q_{12}xY+q_{22}Y^2$ has
\begin{align*}
q_{00}&=\frac{2c+\lambda(e_2^3-3e_2e_3-3e_3^2u+3e_3^2)}{\lambda},\\
q_{01}&=\frac{1+\lambda(3e_2+3e_3u^2-3e_3-1)}{\lambda},\\
q_{11}&=-(u-1)(u^2+u+1),
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align*}
\lambda q_{02}={}&2cz^3+e_2^3\lambda z^3+3e_2^2\lambda uz-3e_2^2\lambda z
 -3e_2e_3\lambda uz^2-3e_2e_3\lambda z^3+3e_2e_3\lambda z^2\\
&-3e_2\lambda u+3e_2\lambda-3e_3\lambda u^2-3e_3\lambda uz
 +6e_3\lambda u+3e_3\lambda z-3e_3\lambda-\lambda+1,\\
\lambda q_{12}={}&3e_2\lambda uz^2+3e_2\lambda z^3-3e_2\lambda z^2
 +2\lambda u^3+3\lambda u^2z-3\lambda u^2-3\lambda uz\\
&-\lambda z^3+\lambda+z^3,\\
\lambda q_{22}={}&-\lambda u^3-3\lambda u^2z+3\lambda u^2-3\lambda uz^2

\label{eq:appendix-determinant-expansion}
\end{equation}
